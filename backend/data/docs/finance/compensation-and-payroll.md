# Compensation and Payroll

This document explains how compensation works at GSVH Corp: exempt versus non-exempt status, overtime, the payroll schedule and payslips, pay bands and reviews, bonuses, and the basics of payroll deductions. It applies to all employees. For reimbursement of business costs, see the finance expenses and reimbursement policy.

## 1. Compensation Status: Exempt and Non-Exempt

Every role is classified as either exempt or non-exempt. Non-exempt employees are eligible for overtime pay and must record all hours worked. Exempt employees are paid a fixed salary for the job regardless of hours and are not eligible for overtime; exemption depends on the role's duties and salary level, not on job title alone.

Classification is determined by HR and Finance together when a role is created and is reviewed when duties change materially. If you believe your classification is incorrect, raise it with HR — misclassification has legal consequences for the company, so we take these queries seriously.

## 2. Overtime

Non-exempt employees who work more than 40 hours in a workweek are paid overtime at 1.5 times their regular hourly rate for the additional hours. Overtime must be approved by your manager in advance; working unapproved overtime is a conduct issue, but the company still pays for hours actually worked, as the law requires.

Time worked is recorded in the timekeeping system and approved weekly by the manager. Overtime is calculated per workweek and is not averaged across two weeks. Exempt employees do not receive overtime but are expected to manage their own schedules to deliver their responsibilities.

## 3. Payroll Schedule and Payslips

Employees are paid semi-monthly, on the 15th and the last business day of each month. When a pay date falls on a weekend or public holiday, payment is made on the preceding business day. Pay covers the period ending a few days before the pay date to allow time for processing.

Each payslip itemizes gross pay, taxes withheld, benefit and retirement contributions, and any other deductions, ending with net pay. Payslips are available in the payroll self-service portal. Review your payslip each period and report any discrepancy to Finance within 30 days so it can be corrected promptly.

## 4. Pay Bands and Salary Reviews

Each role sits in a pay band defined by Finance and HR, based on market benchmarking and internal equity. A band has a minimum, midpoint, and maximum; where you sit within the band reflects experience and sustained performance. Bands are reviewed annually against market data.

Salary reviews happen once a year, aligned to the performance cycle. A review may result in a merit increase, a market adjustment, or no change. A promotion to a higher-banded role is handled separately from the annual merit cycle and may occur at any time. Compensation decisions are confidential between the employee, their manager, HR, and Finance.

## 5. Bonuses

The company operates a discretionary annual bonus tied to company performance and individual contribution. The target bonus for a role is expressed as a percentage of base salary and is communicated in the offer letter. The actual payout depends on the company hitting its financial targets and on the employee's performance rating, and can range from zero to above target.

Some roles, such as sales, have a separate commission or variable-pay plan documented in a plan agreement. Referral bonuses are governed by the HR employment basics policy, not this document. Bonuses are taxable income and are processed through payroll with applicable withholding.

## 6. Deductions and Taxes

The company withholds income tax and statutory contributions from each paycheck as required by law, along with any voluntary deductions you have authorized — for example, additional retirement contributions or benefit premiums. Statutory deductions are not optional. Voluntary deductions can be changed through the payroll self-service portal during the periods the portal allows.

If you change address, marital status, or the number of dependents, update your tax details promptly, because these affect withholding. Finance cannot give individual tax advice; consult a qualified tax professional for your personal situation.

## 7. Final Pay

When employment ends, final pay includes salary earned through the last working day plus any accrued, unused PTO that is payable under the time-off policy and applicable law. Final pay is issued on the next regular pay date unless local law requires sooner. Any outstanding advances, unreturned-equipment charges, or other amounts owed to the company may be deducted from final pay where permitted by law. Offboarding is coordinated with HR, and the internal termination procedure governs timing.

## 8. Pay Confidentiality and Errors

Individual pay is confidential. Employees are free to discuss their own pay, but managers and Finance must keep others' compensation confidential. If a payroll error occurs — an overpayment or underpayment — Finance corrects it in the next cycle. Underpayments are corrected promptly; overpayments are recovered through a reasonable, agreed repayment arrangement rather than a single deduction, unless the amount is small.
