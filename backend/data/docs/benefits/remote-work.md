# Remote Work and Commuting

This document explains how remote and hybrid work operate at Northwind Labs: eligibility, expectations, the home-office equipment stipend, and commuting and parking benefits. It applies to all employees whose roles can be performed remotely. The security requirements for working away from the office are in the IT acceptable use and equipment policies, and business travel costs are in the finance expenses policy.

## 1. Work From Home (WFH) and Hybrid Working

Many roles at the company can be done remotely (WFH) some or all of the time. Whether a role is on-site, hybrid, or fully remote depends on the nature of the work and is agreed between the employee, the manager, and HR. Hybrid is the common pattern: a mix of office and home days, with the office days chosen to maximize collaboration.

Working remotely is a way of working, not a separate set of rules. The same performance expectations, working hours, and conduct standards apply wherever you work. Be reachable during core hours, keep your calendar current, and join meetings with video where it helps the team connect.

## 2. Remote Work Eligibility

A role is eligible for remote work when its responsibilities can be met without a constant physical presence, when the employee has a suitable, safe place to work, and when the arrangement does not harm team collaboration or customer service. Some roles — facilities, lab work, certain support shifts — require on-site presence and are not eligible for full remote work.

Remote arrangements are reviewed periodically and can be adjusted if business needs or performance change. A manager who proposes changing an arrangement should explain the reason and give reasonable notice. Disagreements about remote eligibility are mediated by HR.

## 3. Remote Work Expectations

When working remotely, you are responsible for a safe and productive workspace: a reliable internet connection, a setup that supports good posture, and an environment where you can handle confidential information privately. Follow the IT security requirements — use the company VPN, keep your device locked when unattended, and never let household members use your work device.

Communicate proactively. Without the cues of a shared office, good remote teams over-communicate progress and blockers. Agree with your team on the tools and response-time norms you will use, and respect colleagues' working hours across time zones.

## 4. Home-Office Equipment Stipend

To help you set up a safe and effective home workspace, the company provides a one-time home-office equipment stipend for eligible remote and hybrid employees, up to $500, claimed through the finance expenses portal against receipts. Typical eligible items include a desk, an ergonomic chair, a monitor, or a keyboard and mouse. Items purchased with the stipend that are essential equipment may be recorded as company assets and subject to return on leaving; the claim process explains which items this applies to.

This stipend is separate from the company-issued laptop and peripherals provided through IT. If you need IT equipment rather than furniture, request it through the IT service desk rather than the stipend.

## 5. Commuting and Parking

For employees who travel to an office, the company may offer a commuter benefit in some locations, such as a subsidy toward public-transit passes, as published in the benefits portal. Routine commuting costs are otherwise the employee's responsibility and are not reimbursable as business expenses.

On-site parking is limited and is allocated first to company-car drivers and employees with accessibility needs, then by request to facilities, as described in the finance expenses and parking policy. Where the company cannot offer on-site parking, it does not reimburse routine commuting parking, but business-trip parking remains reimbursable against receipts under the expenses policy.

## 6. Wellbeing While Remote

Remote work can blur the line between work and home. Protect that boundary: keep to your agreed hours, take your breaks and PTO, and switch off at the end of the day. Managers should model healthy remote habits and watch for signs of isolation or overwork on their teams. The Employee Assistance Program and wellness initiatives in the health and wellbeing policy are available to remote employees exactly as they are to on-site staff.
