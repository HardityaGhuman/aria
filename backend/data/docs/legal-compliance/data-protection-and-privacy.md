# Data Protection and Privacy Policy — GSVH Corp

This document explains how GSVH Corp classifies, handles, retains, and disposes of information, and what obligations every employee has under applicable data protection law. It applies to all employees, contractors, and anyone who processes GSVH Corp data or personal data on GSVH Corp's behalf. IT security controls are described in `it/acceptable-use-and-security.md`. HR-specific data handling is in `hr/employment-basics.md`.

## 1. Why This Matters

GSVH Corp processes personal data about employees, customers, vendors, and job candidates every day. Personal data is information that identifies or can identify a living individual. Getting data protection wrong — whether through a breach, improper sharing, or excessive retention — causes real harm to the people whose data it is, and exposes the company to regulatory penalties, reputational damage, and litigation.

Data protection is not a compliance checkbox. It is a commitment to treat people's information with the same care we would want applied to our own.

## 2. Data Classification

GSVH Corp classifies all information into four categories. Every employee must understand these categories and apply them to data they create, receive, or handle.

| Class | Label | Definition | Examples |
|-------|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Public** | Public | Information approved for unrestricted external sharing. | Press releases, published job postings, the company website. |
| **Internal** | Internal | Information intended for GSVH Corp employees generally, with no material risk from wider disclosure. | Internal announcements, team wikis, general process documents. |
| **Confidential** | Confidential | Information shared on a need-to-know basis; disclosure outside the company or to unauthorised employees would cause harm. | Compensation data, unreleased product plans, vendor contracts, personnel records. |
| **Restricted** | Restricted | The most sensitive category; access is strictly limited and requires specific authorization. Disclosure would cause serious harm (legal, financial, or safety). | Health data, government-issued ID numbers, 401(k)/pension details, security credentials, pre-IPO financial data. |

When in doubt, classify up. A document you are unsure about should be treated as Confidential until you confirm otherwise with your manager or Legal.

## 3. Principles for Handling Personal Data

When processing personal data (data that identifies or could identify a person), apply these principles:

1. **Lawful basis:** Only collect and process personal data when there is a lawful basis — for example, it is necessary to perform the employment contract, required by law, or you have the individual's freely given, specific, informed consent.
2. **Purpose limitation:** Use personal data only for the specific purpose for which it was collected. Do not repurpose it without reassessing the lawful basis.
3. **Data minimisation:** Collect only the data you actually need. Avoid collecting fields "just in case."
4. **Accuracy:** Keep personal data accurate and up to date. Correct inaccuracies promptly when you become aware of them.
5. **Storage limitation:** Do not keep personal data longer than necessary. Apply the retention schedule in Section 5.
6. **Security:** Protect personal data with appropriate technical and organisational measures. Restrict access to those who need it.
7. **Accountability:** Be prepared to demonstrate that your data handling complies with these principles. Document decisions, keep records of processing.

## 4. Employee Obligations

Every GSVH Corp employee who handles data is personally responsible for:

- Classifying data they create at the correct level and applying appropriate access controls.
- Not sharing Confidential or Restricted data with anyone outside the company (or outside the authorised circle internally) without Legal approval.
- Not copying Restricted or Confidential data to personal devices, personal cloud storage (Google Drive personal, Dropbox personal, etc.), or removable media, unless explicitly authorized.
- Reporting a suspected or confirmed data breach to IT immediately — do not attempt to investigate or remediate a breach yourself. Contact the IT service desk immediately; the breach response process is coordinated by IT and Security.
- Completing the mandatory annual data protection training in the LMS.

Violations of this policy — sharing data without authorisation, circumventing access controls, failing to report a breach — are conduct violations subject to disciplinary action up to and including termination.

## 5. Retention Schedule

Retain data for the minimum period necessary and then delete or anonymise it securely. The default retention period for business records at GSVH Corp is **7 years** from the date of creation or from the end of the relevant business relationship, whichever is later. This default covers contracts, financial records, correspondence, and business records that may be needed for audit, dispute, or regulatory purposes.

Category-specific retention rules override the default where applicable:

| Data Type | Retention Period | Authority |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Employment records (personnel file) | Duration of employment + 7 years | HR / Legal |
| Payroll and tax records | 7 years from the end of the tax year | Finance / Legal |
| Candidate data (rejected applicants) | 24 months from rejection | HR / Legal |
| Customer personal data | Duration of the customer relationship + 3 years, unless law requires longer | Legal |
| Security logs and access logs | 12 months | IT |
| CCTV footage | 30 days unless relevant to an incident | IT / Facilities |
| Health / medical data | Special category: minimum 10 years; consult Legal before deleting | HR / Legal |

When a retention period expires, data must be deleted or anonymised. Hard copy documents are shredded; electronic data is deleted from primary and backup systems. Do not keep data "just in case." Legal holds override the retention schedule — if Legal notifies you of a legal hold, do not delete any data in scope until the hold is lifted.

## 6. Data Subject Rights

Individuals whose personal data we hold have rights under applicable data protection law (GDPR for EU/UK-resident individuals, CCPA for California residents, IT Act and DPDP Act for India-resident individuals). Key rights include:

- **Access:** the right to request a copy of their personal data we hold.
- **Correction:** the right to have inaccurate data corrected.
- **Erasure:** the right to request deletion of their data (subject to overriding legal obligations).
- **Restriction:** the right to request that processing be restricted.
- **Portability:** the right to receive their data in a portable format.

If an individual makes a data rights request — whether an employee, a customer, or a third party — forward it to Legal at `privacy@gsvhcorp.com` within 1 business day. Do not respond to the request directly. Legal coordinates the response within the statutory deadline (typically 30 days from receipt under GDPR; shorter under some regulations).

## 7. Cross-Border Data Transfers

GSVH Corp operates in the US and India, and data regularly moves between entities and jurisdictions. Cross-border transfers of Restricted or personal data require Legal review to ensure an adequate transfer mechanism is in place (e.g., Standard Contractual Clauses for EU data, or the Standard Contractual Clauses under India's DPDP framework). Do not establish new data flows that send personal data to a new country or vendor without checking with Legal first.

## 8. Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA)

Before starting a new project, product, or system that processes Restricted data or large volumes of personal data, conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) in consultation with Legal. A DPIA identifies the risks of the proposed processing and documents the mitigations. DPIAs are required by GDPR for high-risk processing and are GSVH Corp policy for any new processing that is materially different from existing practice. The DPIA template is on the Legal intranet page.

## 9. Vendor Data Processing

When engaging a vendor who will process personal data on GSVH Corp's behalf, a **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)** must be executed before data is shared. Procurement will not onboard a vendor with data-processing responsibilities without a completed DPA. The DPA template is maintained by Legal; use it without modification unless Legal approves an alternative.

## 10. Contact

For suspected breaches, contact the IT service desk immediately by phone (number on the IT intranet page). GSVH Corp's designated Data Protection Officer (DPO) for GDPR purposes is the General Counsel; contact details are on the Legal intranet page.
