# Termination and Discipline — Internal HR Procedure

This is an internal HR procedure document. It describes how managers and HR handle progressive discipline, performance improvement plans, resignation, forced resignation, termination, and references. It is restricted to HR and people managers because it contains the internal decision criteria, notice obligations, and severance guidance that govern how the company ends an employment relationship. It is not a general employee-facing policy.

## 1. Principles

Discipline is corrective, not punitive. The goal is to give an employee a fair, documented opportunity to meet expectations before the company considers ending employment. Apply this procedure consistently across employees in comparable situations; inconsistent application creates legal and fairness risk. Document every step contemporaneously — a decision that is not written down is, for practical purposes, a decision that did not happen.

## 2. Progressive Discipline Steps

Progressive discipline normally moves through four escalating steps. Skip steps only for serious misconduct, and document the reason for skipping.

1. **Verbal warning.** The manager discusses the issue privately, states the expectation clearly, and agrees on a timeline for improvement. The manager records the date and substance of the conversation in the employee's file.
2. **Written warning.** If the issue continues, HR and the manager issue a written warning describing the conduct, the prior verbal warning, the required improvement, and the consequence of further failure. The employee signs to acknowledge receipt — signing acknowledges receipt, not agreement.
3. **Final written warning / suspension.** A final warning, optionally with a short unpaid or paid suspension pending review, signals that continued failure will lead to termination.
4. **Termination.** Where prior steps have not produced sustained improvement, employment is ended following the process in section 5.

Each step requires a documented conversation and a clear, time-bound improvement expectation. The employee may bring a colleague to any formal disciplinary meeting.

## 3. Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

A PIP is used for performance shortfalls rather than conduct violations. A PIP runs for 30 to 60 days and must specify measurable objectives, the support the company will provide, check-in dates, and the consequence of not meeting the objectives. HR reviews and approves every PIP before it is issued.

A PIP is a genuine opportunity to improve, not a pre-determined path to exit. If the objectives are met, the PIP closes successfully and is noted in the file. If they are partly met, the manager and HR may extend the PIP once. A PIP must never be used to manage out an employee for an unlawful reason; HR screens every PIP for that risk.

## 4. Resignation and Forced Resignation

An employee who resigns should give written notice consistent with their contract — typically two weeks for individual contributors and four weeks for managers. HR conducts an exit interview and coordinates the offboarding checklist (access revocation, asset return, final pay).

Forced resignation — pressuring an employee to resign in lieu of termination — is not permitted as an informal workaround. If the company has grounds to terminate, it follows the termination process. If it does not, it may not manufacture a resignation. A resignation obtained through coercion can be treated in law as a constructive dismissal, exposing the company to the same liability as an unlawful termination, so route any such situation through HR before acting.

## 5. Termination

Termination requires HR sign-off and, except in cases of gross misconduct, completion of the progressive discipline steps. Before approving a termination, HR verifies that: the documentation supports the decision; comparable cases were handled consistently; the reason is lawful and non-discriminatory; and all notice and final-pay obligations are identified.

Gross misconduct — violence, theft, fraud, serious harassment, or a serious safety breach — may justify immediate termination without prior steps, but still requires a documented investigation and HR approval. On the termination date, HR coordinates with IT to revoke system access at the agreed time, recovers company equipment, and issues final pay including any accrued unused PTO as required by law and the compensation policy.

## 6. Notice and Severance

Notice periods follow the contract and applicable law, whichever is greater. Where the company ends employment without cause, it may offer severance in exchange for a signed release of claims. Severance guidance is one week of base pay per completed year of service, subject to a minimum of two weeks and HR discretion based on tenure, role, and circumstances. Severance terms are confidential and are approved by HR and the relevant department head.

## 7. References

The company provides neutral references only: confirmation of job title, dates of employment, and — with the former employee's written consent — eligibility for rehire. Managers must not give personal, ad-hoc references on company letterhead or imply they speak for the company. Route all reference requests to HR. This protects both the former employee and the company from claims arising out of subjective statements.

## 8. Documentation and Retention

All disciplinary records, PIPs, termination decisions, and severance agreements are retained in the confidential personnel file for the period required by law. Access is limited to HR and, on a need-to-know basis, the employee's management chain. These records are treated as sensitive personal data under the company's data protection commitments.
