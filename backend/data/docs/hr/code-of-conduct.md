# Employee Code of Conduct

This Code of Conduct describes the behavior the company expects from everyone at Northwind Labs — employees, contractors, volunteers, vendors, and stakeholders. It covers dress code, conflicts of interest, relationships at work, workplace visitors, solicitation, harassment and violence, and workplace safety and health. These policies help us build a productive, lawful, and pleasant workplace. They apply to all employees regardless of department.

## 1. Dress Code

Our dress code is business casual unless a client meeting, event, or safety requirement calls for something more formal. Use good judgment: clothing should be clean, professional, and appropriate for a shared workplace. Employees in laboratory or facilities roles must wear the protective equipment specified for their area at all times.

We do not impose dress requirements that discriminate on the basis of gender, religion, or ethnicity. Reasonable accommodations for religious dress and grooming are always granted. If you are unsure whether an outfit is appropriate for a specific occasion, ask your manager in advance.

## 2. Conflict of Interest

A conflict of interest arises when your personal interests could interfere — or appear to interfere — with your ability to act in the company's best interest. Examples include holding a financial stake in a competitor or supplier, accepting significant gifts from a vendor, or steering company business toward a relative's firm.

Disclose any actual or potential conflict to your manager and to HR as soon as you become aware of it. Disclosure is not an admission of wrongdoing; failing to disclose is the violation. Gifts from vendors or clients worth more than $100 must be declined or reported. Outside employment is permitted as long as it does not compete with the company, does not use company resources, and does not interfere with your job.

## 3. Employee Relationships and Fraternization

We do not prohibit consensual personal relationships between colleagues, but we do require that they never compromise fairness, supervision, or the work environment. A manager must never be in a direct reporting relationship with someone they are dating. If such a situation arises, disclose it to HR so reporting lines can be adjusted.

Fraternization that disrupts work, creates a real or perceived favoritism, or leads to harassment will be addressed through the discipline process. Romantic advances that continue after the other person has signaled they are unwelcome are treated as harassment, covered below.

## 4. Employment of Relatives

Relatives may work at the company, but not in a direct reporting line, and not where one relative can influence the other's hiring, compensation, performance review, or discipline. Hiring managers must disclose a family relationship with any candidate before that candidate enters the hiring process. Decisions involving a relative must be made by an uninvolved manager with HR oversight.

## 5. Workplace Visitors

Visitors must sign in at reception, wear a visitor badge, and be accompanied by an employee at all times in non-public areas. Do not give visitors access to confidential information, restricted systems, or secured areas. Employees are responsible for the conduct of their visitors. For deliveries and contractors, follow the facilities check-in procedure and confirm identity before granting access.

## 6. Solicitation and Distribution

Employees may not solicit colleagues for commercial, political, or charitable causes during working time, and may not distribute literature in work areas during working time. Non-working time — breaks and meal periods — in non-work areas is fine. Non-employees may not solicit or distribute on company premises at any time. Company-sponsored charitable drives are an exception and are coordinated through HR.

## 7. Harassment and Violence

We have zero tolerance for harassment of any kind, including sexual harassment, bullying, and intimidation. Harassment is any unwelcome conduct — verbal, physical, or visual — that creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment, or that is made a condition of employment.

If you experience or witness harassment, report it to your manager, to HR, or through the confidential reporting line. Reports are taken seriously, investigated promptly, and handled as confidentially as possible. The company strictly prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports harassment in good faith or participates in an investigation.

Workplace violence — threats, physical aggression, or intimidation — is grounds for immediate removal from the premises and may result in termination and referral to law enforcement. If you feel unsafe, contact security or call emergency services first, then notify HR.

## 8. Workplace Safety and Health

Everyone is responsible for keeping the workplace safe. Follow posted safety rules, report hazards to facilities promptly, and never bypass a safety control. Know the location of fire exits, extinguishers, and first-aid kits in your area.

Preventative action is the priority: we conduct regular risk assessments, maintain equipment on schedule, and provide safety training for roles that need it. In an emergency, follow the evacuation plan, move to the designated assembly point, and wait for an all-clear from the emergency coordinator. Report all accidents and near-misses to HR and facilities, even minor ones, so we can prevent a recurrence.

## 9. Drug-Free Workplace and Smoking

The workplace is drug-free. Reporting to work under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs is prohibited, as is the use, possession, or sale of illegal drugs on company premises. Employees taking prescribed medication that may affect their work should inform their manager confidentially so reasonable adjustments can be made. Smoking, including vaping, is permitted only in designated outdoor areas.

## 10. Enforcement

Violations of this Code are handled through the progressive discipline process, except where the severity of the conduct — such as violence, theft, or serious harassment — warrants immediate termination. Managers apply this Code consistently; selective enforcement is itself a conduct issue. Questions about how the Code applies to a specific situation should go to HR.
