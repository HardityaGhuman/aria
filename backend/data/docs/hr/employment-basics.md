# Employment Basics

This document defines the basic employment policies at Northwind Labs: the contract types we offer, our commitment to equal opportunity, how we recruit and select candidates, background checks, the employee referral program, attendance expectations, and the probation period for new hires. It applies to all employees, contractors, and hiring managers.

## 1. Employment Contract Types

Full-time employees work at least 30 hours per week, or 130 hours per month on average. Part-time employees work fewer than 30 hours per week. Both full-time and part-time employees may hold either a temporary (fixed-term) or an indefinite-duration contract.

Full-time employees on an indefinite-duration contract are entitled to the company's full benefits package, including health coverage, paid time off accrual, and retirement contributions. Part-time and temporary employees receive a prorated subset of benefits based on scheduled hours; see the Benefits and Perks documents for the exact eligibility thresholds.

Temporary contracts have a defined end date and may be renewed once before the company must decide whether to convert the role to indefinite duration. Independent contractors are engaged through a separate services agreement and are not covered by this handbook.

## 2. Equal Opportunity Employment

Northwind Labs is an equal opportunity employer. We do not tolerate discrimination against protected characteristics including gender, age, sexual orientation, race, nationality, ethnicity, religion, disability, or veteran status. We expect all employees, including executives and HR staff, to treat others with respect and professionalism.

In practice this means we hire and promote people based on skills, experience, and potential, and we work to reduce bias in every process — for example, through structured interviews with predefined scoring rubrics. We make reasonable accommodations so people with disabilities can move safely about our premises and use our products, services, and equipment. We use inclusive, diversity-sensitive language in all official documents, signs, and job advertisements, and we run regular diversity and communication training.

We commit to penalizing every discriminatory, offensive, or inappropriate behavior. Report any discriminatory action against yourself or a colleague to HR. The company will not retaliate against anyone who files a complaint or a discrimination claim. Any employee who retaliates or discriminates will face disciplinary action up to and including termination.

## 3. Recruitment and Selection Process

Our hiring steps vary by role, but we always aim for a process that is fair and effective. A typical hiring cycle moves through these stages: identify the need for a new role and obtain budget approval; write an inclusive job description; source candidates internally and externally; screen applications; conduct structured interviews; run background checks and reference checks; select the most suitable candidate; and make an official offer.

Steps may overlap, and teams may skip steps when appropriate. Each member of a hiring team usually has a distinct responsibility — recruiters source candidates while hiring managers conduct the technical interviews. Throughout the process we aim to keep candidates informed, communicate clearly, and give everyone an equal opportunity. Ask a recruiter for help whenever you need to improve candidate experience or write a more inclusive job description.

Internal candidates are encouraged to apply for open roles. A manager may not block an internal transfer solely to retain an employee; transfer disputes are mediated by HR.

## 4. Background Checks

Background checks are sensitive and we must always comply with applicable law and ensure candidates understand our intentions. As a general rule, commission a background check for finalists only, never for every applicant. Use the company's contracted screening provider, and obtain the candidate's written permission before initiating any check.

The scope of a check is limited to what is relevant to the role. For most positions this means verifying employment history and education. Roles with financial authority or access to sensitive systems may additionally require a credit or criminal records check, where permitted by law. If a check surfaces adverse information, HR conducts an individualized assessment before any decision — an automatic rejection based solely on a record is not permitted.

## 5. Employee Referrals

If you know someone who would be a good fit, refer them. If we hire your referred candidate, you are eligible for a $3,000 referral bonus. Referral rewards may be higher for hard-to-fill roles — for example, a successful referral for a Data Scientist position may earn $6,000.

To qualify for a reward, the referred candidate must not have applied to the company within the previous year, and must be hired as a permanent full-time or part-time employee rather than as a temporary worker or contractor. Referrers remain eligible for rewards even if the candidate is hired later or for a different position than the one originally referred. Rewards may be subject to taxation; contact HR or the referral program manager for details.

Submit referrals through the internal referral portal or directly to the Talent Acquisition team. We encourage you to consider your professional and external networks as a source of strong candidates.

## 6. Attendance

We expect you to be present during your scheduled working hours. If an emergency prevents you from coming to work, contact your manager as soon as possible. We will excuse unreported absences only in cases of serious accidents or acute medical emergencies. Whenever possible, give your manager advance notice if you will not be coming in.

Repeated unexplained absence is treated as a performance and conduct issue and is handled through the progressive discipline process described in the HR termination and discipline policy. Patterns of tardiness should first be addressed informally between the employee and manager before any formal step is taken.

## 7. Probation Period

New hires serve a probation period of 90 calendar days from their start date. During probation, both the employee and the company can end the relationship with a shorter notice period — one week rather than the standard notice — as the role is still being evaluated on both sides.

Managers should hold a structured check-in at the 30-day and 60-day marks and a formal review before the probation period ends. A probation period may be extended once, by up to 30 days, where more time is genuinely needed to assess fit; the extension and its reasons must be documented and shared with the employee. Successful completion of probation is confirmed in writing and does not change the employee's contract type.

## 8. Personnel Records

HR maintains a confidential personnel file for every employee containing the signed contract, tax and payroll forms, performance reviews, and any disciplinary records. Employees may request to review their own file by contacting HR. Personnel data is processed in line with the data protection commitments in the IT acceptable use and security policy, and is never shared with third parties except as required by law or with the employee's consent.
