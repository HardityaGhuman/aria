# Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) Procedure — GSVH Corp

This document is intended for managers and HR Business Partners. It defines when and how a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) is used at GSVH Corp, the structure and duration options, the check-in cadence, and the outcomes. A PIP is a formal, documented process — it is not a prelude to termination by default; used correctly, it is a genuine tool for returning an employee to satisfactory performance.

## 1. When a PIP Is Appropriate

GSVH Corp uses a **1–5 performance rating scale** in its semi-annual performance reviews:

| Rating | Label |
|--------|-------|
| 5 | Exceptional |
| 4 | Exceeds Expectations |
| 3 | Meets Expectations |
| 2 | Partially Meets Expectations |
| 1 | Does Not Meet Expectations |

A PIP is typically initiated when an employee receives a rating of **1** in a formal performance review, or when a manager, in consultation with HR, determines that sustained, documented performance issues have not resolved through informal coaching (typically after 4–6 weeks of informal support).

A PIP is never the first tool. Before initiating a PIP, the manager must have:
- Delivered clear, documented feedback about the performance gap.
- Given the employee a fair and reasonable period to improve (typically 4–6 weeks) with explicit expectations.
- Consulted with an HR Business Partner to confirm the PIP is appropriate.

A PIP is not appropriate for conduct issues (use the disciplinary process in `hr/termination-and-discipline.md`), nor for role changes driven by business restructuring (use the separation procedure).

## 2. PIP Durations

PIPs at GSVH Corp run for one of three standard durations, chosen based on the nature and severity of the performance gap:

- **30-day PIP:** Used when the gap is narrow and specific — for example, a single skill or behaviour that needs rapid correction. Appropriate when the manager has high confidence the employee can close the gap quickly, or where a probation-related improvement is needed in a short window.

- **60-day PIP:** The default duration for most performance gaps. Provides enough time for meaningful improvement across multiple competencies while keeping the period tight enough to maintain urgency.

- **90-day PIP:** Used for complex, multi-dimensional performance gaps where the employee needs time to build fundamentals — for example, a recently promoted employee struggling to operate at the new level. Requires HR Director approval to ensure the longer duration is genuinely justified.

The duration is set at the start and is not shortened without HR approval, even if the employee appears to be improving ahead of schedule. Reducing the duration prematurely can expose the company to claims that the process was not genuine.

## 3. PIP Structure

Every PIP must contain the following elements, documented in writing and signed by the manager, the employee, and the HR Business Partner:

1. **Description of the performance gap** — specific, observable, measurable statements of where performance falls short. Avoid vague characterizations; cite specific incidents with dates.
2. **Expected standard** — what "meets expectations" looks like in concrete, measurable terms for this role and level. Reference the relevant section of the career framework (`people-career/career-framework-overview.md`).
3. **Improvement actions** — the specific steps the employee will take and the resources the company will provide (coaching, training, adjusted workload, mentorship). The company's obligations are as important as the employee's.
4. **Success criteria** — how and when improvement will be measured. Tie each criterion to a specific check-in date.
5. **Consequences** — a clear statement that failure to meet the success criteria by the end of the PIP period may result in further disciplinary action up to and including termination.

The HR Business Partner reviews the PIP document for legal compliance and internal consistency before it is presented to the employee.

## 4. Weekly Check-Ins

**Weekly check-ins are mandatory for the duration of every PIP.** Each check-in is scheduled in advance (not cancelled), runs for 30–60 minutes, and is documented in writing by the manager within 24 hours.

Check-in records must include:
- Date and attendees.
- Progress against each success criterion since the last check-in.
- Specific examples of positive progress and of continuing gaps.
- Actions agreed for the following week.
- Employee's own comments (captured in the notes, even if brief).

The written check-in record is shared with the employee after each session. These records are the evidence base for the outcome decision; incomplete check-in documentation is a process failure that can invalidate the PIP and expose the company to legal risk.

If an employee is absent (e.g., sick leave) during a PIP, the PIP clock pauses for the duration of the absence. The HR Business Partner extends the PIP end date accordingly and documents this in writing to the employee.

## 5. Mid-PIP HR Review

At the midpoint of any 60-day or 90-day PIP, the manager and HR Business Partner meet (without the employee) to review progress. This review assesses:
- Whether the check-in documentation is complete and accurate.
- Whether the company's commitments (coaching, resources) have been delivered.
- Whether the trajectory suggests the employee is likely to succeed or not.
- Whether the PIP duration or success criteria need to be adjusted (adjustments require HR Director approval and must be communicated to the employee in writing).

## 6. Outcomes

At the end of the PIP period, there are three possible outcomes:

**Successful completion:** The employee has met all success criteria. HR issues a written confirmation, the PIP is closed, and the employee returns to the normal performance review cadence. The PIP record is retained in the personnel file but does not automatically affect the next performance rating. The manager schedules a post-PIP support plan (typically 3 months of heightened check-in frequency) to prevent regression.

**Partial improvement:** Some criteria are met but not all. This is not a defined outcome in isolation; the manager and HR must determine whether to extend the PIP (requires HR Director approval; maximum one extension per PIP) or move to disciplinary proceedings. Vague "partial" outcomes are not permitted — each criterion must be assessed as met or not met.

**Unsuccessful completion:** The employee has not met one or more success criteria by the end of the PIP. The manager and HR Business Partner review the documentation and, if the process has been followed correctly, initiate the separation procedure under `hr/termination-and-discipline.md`. The employee receives the standard notice period.

## 7. Confidentiality

PIP documentation is strictly confidential to the employee, the manager, the HR Business Partner, and — where a disciplinary outcome is reached — the HR Director and Legal. The employee's peers and skip-level managers are not informed that a PIP is in progress. Managers must not discuss an active PIP with their peers, other direct reports, or anyone outside the approved circle.

The employee has the right to see all documentation related to their own PIP, including check-in notes. They may bring an HR representative or, in some jurisdictions, a colleague or union representative to any formal PIP meeting; the HR Business Partner confirms the local entitlement before the PIP is opened.

## 8. The Appraisal (Performance Review) Connection

PIPs are separate from, but informed by, the semi-annual **performance review** (appraisal) cycle. An active PIP supersedes the normal appraisal for that cycle; the PIP outcome is the primary performance record for the period. A rating of 1 or 2 in an appraisal does not automatically trigger a PIP — the manager must still follow the informal coaching steps in Section 1. Conversely, a completed PIP (whether successful or not) feeds into the next appraisal as a data point, not as a final verdict.
