# Company Equipment and Devices

This document covers the hardware side of IT at GSVH Corp: company-issued equipment, bring-your-own-device (BYOD), software installation, VPN and remote access, and returning equipment when you change roles or leave. It applies to everyone who is issued company hardware or uses a personal device for work. Acceptable use and security of data are covered in the IT acceptable use and security policy.

## 1. Company-Issued Equipment

The company provides the equipment you need to do your job — typically a laptop, and where appropriate a monitor, phone, or peripherals. Equipment remains company property. Take reasonable care of it: keep it physically secure, do not leave it visible in a car, and use a protective case for laptops that travel. Report loss, theft, or damage to the IT service desk immediately so the device can be locked or wiped remotely.

Each device is enrolled in the company's device-management system, which enforces disk encryption, automatic security updates, and endpoint protection. Do not attempt to remove the management profile, disable encryption, or run the device as a local administrator unless IT has granted you that right for a specific reason.

## 2. Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)

You may access limited company services — email, calendar, chat — from a personal phone or tablet, provided the device meets minimum security requirements: a current operating system, a screen lock, and the company's mobile-management profile. The profile separates work data from personal data and lets IT wipe only the work container if the device is lost or you leave the company; it does not give IT access to your personal photos, messages, or apps.

Personal computers should not be used for company work that involves confidential data. If you have a genuine need to work from a personal computer, raise it with IT so an approved, secured option can be arranged rather than copying data onto an unmanaged machine.

## 3. Software Installation

Install software only from the company's approved software catalog or with IT approval. Unapproved software can introduce security vulnerabilities, licensing violations, and compatibility problems. Do not install pirated software, and do not use personal software licenses for company work or company licenses for personal use.

If you need a tool that is not in the catalog, request it through the IT service desk with a short business justification. IT reviews requests for security and licensing and will provision an approved equivalent where the exact tool is not suitable.

## 4. VPN and Remote Access

When working away from the office, connect to company systems through the company VPN or the approved zero-trust access tool. Do not access internal systems over public Wi-Fi without the VPN, and never use an unapproved third-party VPN to reach company resources. The VPN encrypts your connection and applies the same security controls you would have on-site.

Remote access is tied to your identity and your enrolled device. Multi-factor authentication applies to remote access just as it does to email. If remote access stops working, contact the IT service desk rather than seeking an unofficial workaround.

## 5. Printing and Peripherals

Use company printers for business documents and collect printed material promptly, especially anything confidential. Do not connect unapproved USB devices or external drives to company machines, because they are a common route for malware and data loss. Approved peripherals are available through the IT service desk.

## 6. Equipment Return on Exit or Role Change

When you leave the company, change to a role that no longer needs certain equipment, or are asked to return a device for any reason, you must return all company equipment in good working condition. Offboarding is coordinated between IT, HR, and your manager: IT schedules the device return and access revocation, HR confirms the leaving date, and Finance reconciles any equipment-related amounts.

On the agreed date, IT revokes your system access and either collects the equipment in person or sends a prepaid return kit for remote employees. Equipment that is not returned, or is returned with damage beyond normal wear, may result in a charge through final pay where permitted, as noted in the finance compensation policy. Back up any personal files from your work device before your last day, because the device is wiped on return.

## 7. Asset Records

IT maintains an asset register linking each device to the employee it is issued to. Keep your assigned equipment list accurate by reporting transfers, swaps, and replacements to the IT service desk. Accurate asset records are what make a clean, fast offboarding possible and reduce disputes about what was issued and returned.
