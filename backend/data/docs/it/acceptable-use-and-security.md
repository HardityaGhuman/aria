# Acceptable Use and Cyber Security

This document sets out how to use the company's digital systems safely and responsibly at GSVH Corp: internet and email use, cell phones, social media, passwords and multi-factor authentication, data protection and confidentiality, and how to report a security incident. It applies to everyone who uses company systems or handles company data. Hardware and devices are covered in the IT equipment and devices policy.

## 1. Acceptable Use Principles

Company systems are provided for work. Incidental personal use is allowed as long as it is reasonable, lawful, does not interfere with your job, and does not consume significant resources. You have no expectation of privacy in your use of company systems; the company may monitor and audit usage where lawful and necessary to protect the business.

Never use company systems to access, store, or share illegal, harassing, or offensive material. Do not attempt to bypass security controls, install unauthorized software, or connect untrusted devices to the corporate network. Report anything that looks wrong to the IT service desk.

## 2. Internet Usage

Browse responsibly. Avoid downloading files from untrusted sites, and never disable the endpoint protection on your device. Streaming, large personal downloads, and bandwidth-heavy personal use during work hours are discouraged because they affect everyone. The company filters known-malicious and inappropriate sites; do not attempt to circumvent the filter with proxies or VPNs that are not company-approved.

## 3. Corporate Email

Use your company email for business communication only, and treat every message as if it could be read by someone outside the company. Do not auto-forward company email to a personal account. Be alert to phishing: check the sender address, hover over links before clicking, and never enter your company password into a page you reached from an email link. If a message asks for credentials, a payment, or urgent secrecy, treat it as suspicious and report it.

Confidential information sent by email should be limited to the recipients who need it, and sensitive attachments should be shared through approved file-sharing rather than as open attachments where possible. Large distribution lists are for genuine announcements, not personal messages.

## 4. Cell Phones

Use personal and company cell phones in a way that does not disrupt colleagues or compromise safety. Do not use a handheld phone while driving a company vehicle. If you access company email or data on a personal phone, the device must have a screen lock and the company mobile-management profile installed, as described in the equipment and devices policy. Keep work data within approved apps.

## 5. Social Media

You are free to use social media in your personal life. When you do, make clear that your views are your own and not the company's. Do not share confidential company information, unreleased product details, customer data, or anything covered by a non-disclosure obligation. Do not speak on behalf of the company unless you are authorized to do so. Harassing or discriminatory posts about colleagues are a conduct matter under the Code of Conduct, even when posted from a personal account on personal time.

## 6. Passwords and Multi-Factor Authentication

Use a unique, strong password for your company account and never reuse it on other sites. The company provides a password manager; use it. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is mandatory for all company accounts — approve MFA prompts only when you initiated the sign-in, and report any prompt you did not trigger, because it may mean someone has your password.

Never share your password or MFA codes with anyone, including IT staff — IT will never ask for them. If you suspect your credentials are compromised, change your password immediately and contact the IT service desk.

## 7. Data Protection and Confidentiality

Protect company and personal data in line with applicable data protection law. Collect and use personal data only for legitimate business purposes, share it only with those who need it, and store it only in approved systems. Classify information by sensitivity and handle the most sensitive categories — personnel records, customer data, financial data — with extra care.

Do not copy confidential data to personal devices, personal cloud storage, or removable media. When data is no longer needed, dispose of it securely following the retention schedule. Confidentiality obligations continue after you leave the company.

## 8. Reporting a Security Incident

If you lose a device, click a suspicious link, suspect a phishing message, or notice anything that could be a breach, report it to the IT service desk immediately — speed limits the damage. There is no penalty for reporting a genuine mistake quickly and honestly; the penalty is for hiding it. The incident response team will guide you on the next steps, such as changing passwords or isolating a device. Do not try to investigate or clean up a suspected breach yourself.
