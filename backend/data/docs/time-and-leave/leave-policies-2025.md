# Leave Policies — GSVH Corp (2025 Version)

This document covered the specific leave types at GSVH Corp beyond ordinary vacation for the policy year beginning 1 January 2025: sick leave, long-term illness, bereavement leave, jury duty and voting leave, and parental, maternity, and paternity leave. It applied to all employees. Ordinary paid time off accrual in this version was **18 days base per year** (see Section 0 and the PTO Accrual note). The 2026 policy increased the base PTO accrual to 20 days; see `time-and-leave/leave-policies.md` for the current entitlement.

## 0. PTO Accrual Under This Version (2025)

Under this version of the leave policy (effective 2025-01-01 through 2025-12-31):
- **Base PTO accrual was 18 days per calendar year**, accruing monthly at 1.5 days per completed month of service.
- The tenure top-up (+1 day per year after Year 3, to a cap of 26 days) applied under this version at the same rate.
- Carryover was permitted up to 5 days.

The 2026.1 policy (`time-and-leave/leave-policies.md`) increased the base to 20 days (monthly accrual 1.67 days) and raised the tenure cap to 28 days. All other leave types in this document are unchanged in the 2026 policy.

## 1. Sick Leave

Employees received paid sick leave for genuine illness or injury that prevented them from working. Full-time employees were entitled to up to 10 paid sick days per calendar year, separate from PTO. Notification to the manager was required on the first day of illness as early as possible; attendance while contagious was discouraged.

For absences of up to two consecutive days, self-certification was sufficient. For absences of three or more consecutive days, the company could ask for a medical note. Sick days were not paid out on leaving and did not carry over; their purpose was to allow recovery without loss of pay.

## 2. Long-Term Illness

For illness or injury extending beyond the normal sick-leave allowance, long-term illness arrangements applied. HR worked with the employee to agree a plan, which could include continued partial pay according to the contract and applicable statutory entitlement, regular check-ins, and a phased return when the employee was ready. The company considered reasonable adjustments (adjusted duties, reduced hours, equipment) in line with its equal opportunity commitments.

## 3. Bereavement Leave

The company provided paid bereavement leave so employees could grieve and handle practical matters. Employees received up to 5 paid days for the death of an immediate family member (spouse or partner, child, parent, or sibling) and up to 2 paid days for an extended family member. Additional time could be arranged through PTO or unpaid leave in consultation with the manager and HR.

## 4. Jury Duty and Voting

Employees summoned for jury duty received paid leave for required service days; the jury summons had to be shared with the manager and HR promptly. Any jury fee received was the employee's to keep. Reasonable paid time was granted for voting where working hours and polling hours overlapped; advance notice to the manager was required.

## 5. Parental Leave

All new parents, regardless of gender, were entitled to parental leave supporting the arrival of a child through birth, adoption, or fostering. Parental leave was in addition to any maternity or paternity entitlement. Employees were required to give HR and the manager reasonable advance notice of intended leave dates.

## 6. Maternity and Paternity Leave

Birthing parents were entitled to maternity leave of at least 16 weeks, with pay per the parental pay policy and applicable statutory entitlement, whichever was greater. Non-birthing parents were entitled to paternity or partner leave of at least 4 weeks, taken flexibly in the months around the child's arrival. Where local law provided greater entitlements, local law applied.

## 7. Requesting and Coordinating Leave

Leave types covered in this policy were requested through HR rather than the ordinary PTO portal. The company did not penalize any employee for taking entitled leave, and retaliation for requesting protected leave was a serious conduct violation. Employees uncertain which leave type applied to their situation were encouraged to ask HR.
