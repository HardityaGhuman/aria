# Working Hours and Paid Time Off

This document explains working hours, attendance recording, paid time off (PTO) accrual and booking, and public holidays at GSVH Corp. It applies to all employees. Sick leave, bereavement, parental leave, and other specific leave types are covered in the separate leave policies document.

## 1. Working Hours

Standard full-time hours are 40 hours per week, normally worked Monday to Friday. Core hours, when most collaboration happens, are 10:00 to 16:00; outside core hours we offer flexible start and finish times by agreement with your manager, as long as you deliver your responsibilities and attend required meetings. Part-time schedules are agreed individually and recorded in the contract.

We trust employees to manage their time. Flexibility is a two-way commitment: it works because people are reachable during core hours and responsive to their teams. Where a role has fixed coverage requirements — for example, support shifts — the team's schedule takes precedence over individual flexibility.

## 2. Attendance and Timekeeping

Be present and reachable during your agreed hours. Non-exempt employees record all hours worked in the timekeeping system, and managers approve timesheets weekly; accurate records are what make correct pay and overtime possible. Exempt employees do not log hourly time but are still expected to keep their calendar current so colleagues know their availability.

If you cannot work as scheduled, tell your manager as early as possible. Brief, occasional appointments during the day are handled informally with your manager and do not normally require booking PTO; longer or recurring absences should be booked as time off.

## 3. Paid Time Off Accrual

Full-time employees accrue 20 days of PTO per year, accruing monthly at 1.67 days per completed month of service. PTO is for vacation and personal time and is separate from sick leave and public holidays. Part-time employees accrue PTO on a prorated basis according to their scheduled hours.

New employees begin accruing PTO from their start date and may book accrued time during probation with manager approval. PTO is shown in the time-off portal, which always reflects your current accrued and booked balances.

## 4. Booking and Approving PTO

Request PTO through the time-off portal, ideally at least two weeks ahead for anything longer than two days, so your manager can plan coverage. Managers approve requests fairly and consistently, balancing the employee's wishes against business needs and other team members' bookings. A manager who declines a request should explain why and, where possible, offer alternative dates.

You are encouraged to use your PTO — time off matters for wellbeing and performance. Managers should actively support people in taking their leave rather than letting balances build up unused.

## 5. Carryover and Payout

You may carry over up to 5 unused PTO days into the next calendar year; days above that are forfeited unless local law requires otherwise, so plan to use your allowance within the year. Carried-over days should be used within the first quarter of the new year.

PTO is not normally paid out while you are employed. On leaving the company, accrued and unused PTO is paid out with final pay to the extent required by law and the compensation policy. The internal HR offboarding procedure coordinates the timing.

## 6. Public Holidays

The company observes the public holidays of the country or region in which you are employed, published each year in the time-off portal. Public holidays are paid and are in addition to your PTO allowance. When a public holiday falls on a weekend, the company follows the local convention for a substitute day where one applies.

Employees required to work on a public holiday for coverage reasons receive either an alternative day off in lieu or premium pay, according to their contract and local law. Where teams observe different regional holidays, plan around colleagues' calendars for scheduling shared work.

## 7. Unpaid Leave

Where you have exhausted PTO and need additional time off for a genuine reason, you may request unpaid leave. Unpaid leave is at the company's discretion, requires manager and HR approval, and is agreed in writing including its duration and the effect on benefits accrual. Statutory leave entitlements — such as parental or sick leave — are handled under the leave policies document and are not treated as discretionary unpaid leave.
