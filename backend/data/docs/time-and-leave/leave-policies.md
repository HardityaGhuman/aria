# Leave Policies

> **Version note:** This is the current policy (2026.1, effective 2026-01-01). Supersedes the 2025 leave policy (§ accrual) — see `time-and-leave/leave-policies-2025.md` for the prior version. The key change: base PTO accrual increased from 18 days (2025) to 20 days (2026).

This document covers the specific leave types at GSVH Corp beyond ordinary vacation: sick leave, long-term illness, bereavement leave, jury duty and voting leave, and parental, maternity, and paternity leave. It applies to all employees. Ordinary paid time off and public holidays are covered in the working hours and PTO document.

## 1. Sick Leave

Employees receive paid sick leave for genuine illness or injury that prevents them from working. Full-time employees are entitled to up to 10 paid sick days per calendar year, separate from PTO. If you are unwell, notify your manager as early as possible on the first day and do not come to work if you are contagious.

For absences of up to two consecutive days, self-certification is enough. For absences of three or more consecutive days, the company may ask for a medical note. Sick days are not paid out on leaving and do not carry over; their purpose is to let you recover without losing pay, not to extend your vacation allowance.

## 2. Long-Term Illness

If an illness or injury keeps you off work beyond the normal sick-leave allowance, you move onto long-term illness arrangements. HR works with you to agree a plan, which may include continued partial pay according to your contract and any statutory entitlement, regular check-ins, and a phased return-to-work when you are ready.

The company will consider reasonable adjustments to help you return — adjusted duties, reduced hours, or equipment — in line with the equal opportunity commitments in the HR policies. Medical information you share is treated as sensitive personal data and is kept confidential within HR and your management chain on a need-to-know basis.

## 3. Bereavement Leave

The company provides paid bereavement leave so you can grieve and handle practical arrangements after a death in the family. Employees receive up to 5 paid days for the death of an immediate family member — a spouse or partner, child, parent, or sibling — and up to 2 paid days for an extended family member.

We recognize that grief does not follow a fixed timetable. If you need more time, talk to your manager and HR about using PTO or unpaid leave. Managers are expected to be compassionate and flexible; documentation is not required to take bereavement leave.

## 4. Jury Duty and Voting

The company supports your civic responsibilities. If you are summoned for jury duty, you receive paid leave for the days you are required to serve; share the summons with your manager and HR as soon as you receive it so coverage can be arranged. Any jury fee you receive is yours to keep.

Where voting hours do not fall outside your working hours, the company grants reasonable paid time to vote. Tell your manager in advance so the team can plan. These provisions follow local law, which may be more generous than this policy; where it is, local law applies.

## 5. Parental Leave

Parental leave supports employees becoming parents through birth, adoption, or fostering. All new parents, regardless of gender, are entitled to parental leave, and the company encourages all parents to take it. Parental leave is in addition to any maternity or paternity entitlement and is taken in the period around and after the arrival of a child, as agreed with HR.

Give HR and your manager reasonable notice of your intended leave dates so the team can plan cover. Your role, or a comparable one, is protected while you are on parental leave, and your benefits continue to accrue according to your contract and local law.

## 6. Maternity and Paternity Leave

Birthing parents are entitled to maternity leave of at least 16 weeks, with pay according to the company's parental pay policy and any statutory entitlement, whichever is greater. Maternity leave can begin before the expected arrival date as advised by a medical professional. The company supports a phased return and provides reasonable facilities for nursing parents on return.

Non-birthing parents are entitled to paternity or partner leave of at least 4 weeks, taken flexibly in the months around the arrival. As with parental leave, the role is protected and benefits continue. Where local law provides greater entitlements than this policy, local law applies. Speak with HR early so your leave, pay, and return are planned smoothly.

## 7. Requesting and Coordinating Leave

Request specific leave types through HR rather than the ordinary PTO portal, because they involve pay rules and statutory entitlements that HR administers. The company never penalizes anyone for taking the leave they are entitled to, and retaliation for requesting protected leave is a serious conduct violation. If you are unsure which leave type applies to your situation, ask HR — they will help you use the right entitlement.
