# Manager Expectations — GSVH Corp

This document defines what GSVH Corp expects of all people managers. It covers the management basics — 1:1s, team size, calibration, career development — and the behaviours that distinguish effective management from mere role assignment. It applies to all employees who manage one or more direct reports. Managers are accountable to these expectations; sustained failures to meet them are addressed through performance management.

## 1. What Managers Are For

At GSVH Corp, managers exist to make their team members more effective and to translate company direction into team action. A manager's output is not their own individual work — it is the collective output of the team they lead. A manager who does excellent individual work but whose team is underperforming, disengaged, or leaving is not doing their job.

Effective management at GSVH Corp requires:
- **Clarity:** team members know what they are working on, why it matters, and what success looks like.
- **Support:** team members have the tools, access, context, and psychological safety they need to do their best work.
- **Accountability:** performance expectations are clear, feedback is regular and direct, and underperformance is addressed — not avoided.
- **Growth:** team members' careers are progressing, not stagnating; managers advocate for their people.

## 2. Team Size (Span of Control)

GSVH Corp's target span of control for people managers is **5 to 8 direct reports**. This range reflects the amount of meaningful management a person can do while maintaining their own individual contribution (at L3–L4) or their own organizational scope (at L5+).

- Fewer than 5 direct reports is generally fine for very senior roles (L5+) where the manager's organizational scope is large. For managers at L3–L4, spans below 4 are reviewed with HR to confirm the structure is appropriate.
- More than 8 direct reports consistently signals that a manager is under-resourced for the management work. Managers consistently above 8 reports should raise this with their skip-level manager and HR to discuss whether additional headcount, reorganization, or a team lead appointment is warranted.
- Temporary deviations (during a hire freeze, parental leave, or a restructuring) are acceptable; sustained oversized spans are not.

## 3. Weekly 1:1s

**Every direct report receives a weekly 1:1 meeting with their manager.** This is the primary mechanism for feedback, career development, problem-solving, and staying connected. 1:1s are the direct report's meeting — they set the agenda, and the manager listens more than they talk.

Minimum standards:
- **Frequency:** Weekly. Bi-weekly is acceptable only for very senior direct reports (L5+) who have explicitly requested it; for L1–L4, weekly is not optional.
- **Duration:** 30 minutes minimum. 45–60 minutes for onboarding employees and during PIPs.
- **Cancellation:** The manager may not cancel a 1:1 without rescheduling it in the same week. Repeated cancellations (more than 2 in a month) are escalated to the skip-level manager in the management quality review.
- **Agenda:** The direct report owns the agenda; the manager may add items but should not commandeer the meeting for status updates.

What productive 1:1s cover:
- Blockers, dependencies, and anything the manager can help remove.
- Work-in-progress feedback (not just finished work).
- Career development: what the report is working toward, what experiences they need.
- Wellbeing: a genuine check-in, not performative.
- Manager's upward and lateral context the report might not otherwise have.

Do not use 1:1s primarily for status updates. If your 1:1s are consistently about "what did you work on this week," you are wasting the meeting.

## 4. Performance Conversations and Feedback

Managers are responsible for delivering ongoing, specific, actionable feedback — not saving it for semi-annual performance reviews.

- **Positive feedback** should be given promptly (within 24–48 hours of the behaviour you want to reinforce) and specifically (name what the person did and why it mattered).
- **Constructive feedback** should be given privately, in the 1:1, not in front of the team or in a public channel. Be direct: vague feedback is unhelpful. Name the specific behaviour, its impact, and what you expect instead.
- **The performance review (appraisal)** is a summary of ongoing feedback, not a surprise. Nothing in a formal performance review should be new to the direct report. If you are writing a 2-rating and the person has not heard that their performance is below expectations before the review conversation, you have failed the process.

The semi-annual performance calibration (see `people-career/career-framework-overview.md`) is where ratings are calibrated across the team and department. Managers must participate in calibration sessions; delegate is not permitted.

## 5. Semi-Annual Calibration

Twice per year (June and December), managers participate in a **calibration session** with their peer managers and HR Business Partner. Calibration is mandatory; managers who miss calibration without an approved leave have their direct reports' ratings deferred to the next cycle.

In calibration, each manager presents their planned ratings and, where applicable, promotion nominations for each direct report. The calibration panel ensures ratings are applied consistently across teams — an "Exceeds" rating in one team reflects the same level of impact as an "Exceeds" in another team. Calibration is not a forced ranking; it is a consistency check.

After calibration, managers deliver the rating conversation to each direct report before any written communication. The manager should explain the rating, the evidence behind it, and the development focus for the next cycle.

## 6. Career Development

Managers are responsible for actively managing the career development of their direct reports, not waiting for reports to ask.

Minimum expectations:
- In each quarterly 1:1 cycle (approximately every 3 months), have a dedicated career development conversation: where does the report want to grow, what is the gap between now and there, and what experiences or projects will close the gap?
- For any direct report who has been at their current level for 18 or more months without a promotion nomination, the manager must be able to articulate either (a) a credible path to readiness or (b) a clear explanation of why the current level is appropriate for the role.
- Managers nominate direct reports for stretch assignments, cross-functional projects, and visibility opportunities. A manager who is hoarding talent (keeping their best people from internal moves) is acting against GSVH Corp's values.

When a direct report is interested in an internal transfer, the manager supports the process. A manager may not block an internal transfer; they may request a handover period of up to 4 weeks to manage continuity.

## 7. Team Wellbeing

Managers are responsible for their team's wellbeing — not as a soft nice-to-have, but as a practical management responsibility. A burned-out team delivers poor work, generates high attrition, and reflects poorly on the manager's judgment.

Practical obligations:
- Respect working hours. Do not send messages at 11pm and expect same-day replies, and actively discourage a culture where doing so earns praise.
- Actively encourage PTO usage. If team members have significant accrued balances, ask why and remove blockers.
- Watch for signs of overload or disengagement in 1:1s and team dynamics. Raise concerns with HR early; a problem caught at 4 weeks is easier to address than one surfaced at 6 months.
- Use the EAP and HR resources proactively, not only reactively.

## 8. Onboarding and Offboarding

**Onboarding:** Managers are responsible for delivering a Day 1 meeting (at least 1 hour), assigning a buddy before the start date, setting a 30/60/90-day onboarding plan in Workday, and conducting structured check-ins at 30 days and 60 days. See `hr/new-joiner-onboarding.md` for the full onboarding process.

**Offboarding:** When a direct report resigns or is separated, the manager initiates the Workday offboarding workflow on or before the last working day. Do not delay the Workday trigger — IT access revocation depends on it (see `it/software-access-and-licenses.md`). Conduct an exit interview where the employee is willing; share themes with HR anonymously.

## 9. What GSVH Corp Managers Do Not Do

The following are failures of management, not stylistic choices:

- **Micromanaging:** Reviewing or approving every individual output rather than setting goals and trusting the team.
- **Avoiding difficult conversations:** Giving vague, positive-only feedback to avoid discomfort; failing to address performance issues because the conversation is uncomfortable.
- **Favouritism:** Consistently advocating for one person's ideas, promotions, or projects over others without a clear merit-based rationale.
- **Taking credit:** Presenting the team's work as individual management achievement rather than crediting the people who did the work.
- **Intimidation or retaliation:** Using position to silence criticism, punish dissent, or retaliate against anyone who raises a complaint. These are conduct violations under `legal-compliance/anti-harassment-and-grievance.md`.

If a direct report raises a concern about management behaviour, HR will treat it seriously, regardless of the manager's seniority or performance record.
